# Visual-direction craft

Use this reference when visual hierarchy, composition, character, or polish is a material part of the task. It provides positive craft criteria. It does not prescribe a universal style.

## Ground the visual intent

Before choosing form, record:

- product job, user moment, and desired attentional sequence;
- approved brand or design-system sources and their revisions;
- reference images or products, with the specific properties they demonstrate;
- platform, content, accessibility, localization, imagery, and production constraints;
- visual decisions already fixed versus axes open to exploration.

Classify a source as an official system, a relevant precedent, an aesthetic family, or an approximation. Separately classify the commissioned intent as inspiration, benchmark, or explicit reproduction. Source class describes what the reference is. Intent describes how it may influence the task. Neither authorizes copying identity, assets, or product behavior. When references conflict, preserve their lineage and ask which authority governs.

## Craft lenses

Apply only the lenses that affect the current outcome. For each lens, describe the intended relationship, evidence in the artifact, failure risk, and what remains an implementation freedom.

### Hierarchy

Make the order of attention match task priority. Use size, weight, placement, contrast, grouping, and disclosure deliberately. Important does not always mean largest. Competing emphasis is a defect when the user cannot tell what to do or understand first.

### Rhythm

Create recurring spatial and typographic intervals that make scanning predictable, then vary them when meaning changes. Evaluate sequences and groups, not isolated gap values. Avoid both accidental inconsistency and mechanical sameness that erases structure.

### Optical balance

Judge perceived weight rather than mathematical centering alone. Account for glyph shapes, icon mass, asymmetrical content, borders, shadows, and neighboring whitespace. Optical correction is valid when it improves the intended alignment without creating unexplained exceptions.

### Composition

Organize dominant, supporting, and quiet regions around the user task. Control alignment, proportion, negative space, layering, and focal movement. A composition should work with realistic content and relevant viewport changes. A single ideal screenshot is insufficient evidence.

### Typography

Define roles, relationships, measure, leading, weight, case, emphasis, numerals, truncation, and fallback behavior from content needs and available type assets. Test actual labels, long content, dense data, localization, zoom, and state changes. Do not invent a type scale or font personality when effective canon already owns it.

### Color relationships

Use color to express hierarchy, grouping, identity, state, and feedback while preserving non-color cues. Evaluate role relationships across backgrounds, themes, interaction states, data density, and accessibility conditions. Avoid isolated color picking detached from surfaces, content, and contrast behavior.

### Density

Choose density based on task frequency, expertise, content volume, error cost, input mode, and viewport. Do not base it on a generic sparse-versus-dense preference. Preserve legibility, target usability, grouping, and scan efficiency. When density varies by mode, specify what changes and what meaning remains stable.

### Imagery and iconography

Give every image, illustration, icon, or texture a job: orientation, explanation, recognition, emotional framing, or identity. Record source, rights or provenance, crop behavior, fallback, alt treatment, localization sensitivity, and dark/theme behavior. Do not fabricate logos, people, testimonials, social proof, or “authentic” product data.

### Affordance

Make interactive elements recognizable through form, label, state, placement, feedback, and platform expectations. Distinctiveness must not hide controls. Cover hover where applicable, focus, pressed, selected, disabled, loading, error, and completed states without relying on color alone.

### Coherence

Keep repeated relationships recognizable across surfaces while allowing motivated exceptions. Check that visual language, interaction behavior, content voice, imagery, and motion tell the same story. A coherent direction can be expressive. It makes exceptions legible and purposeful.

### Distinctiveness

Tie memorable choices to product intent, content, or a useful interaction relationship. Name what is specific and why it belongs. Novelty without product evidence, genre stereotypes, and indiscriminate “premium” styling are not positive craft.

### Polish

Resolve edge conditions that change perceived quality: baseline alignment, icon-text relationships, border joins, clipping, focus treatment, empty/error states, loading transitions, content extremes, theme edges, responsive reflow, and motion finish. Polish refines approved meaning. It must not disguise an unresolved flow or accessibility gap.

## Motion Choice Record

Use this governed evidence record for each material motion relationship. It is never normative authority. Use the existing [Design decision lifecycle](authority-and-status.md#design-decision) for its status: `proposed`, `approved`, `rejected`, `superseded`, or `revoked`. Do not define a separate motion lifecycle. Do not require motion for its own sake or impose a quota.

Observed reference motion is evidence, not a recommendation. `Approved` selects the recorded motion relationship within the cited decision scope. It does not confer implementation effectivity. Implementation authority exists only after the motion requirements enter effective product canon, effective design-system canon, or an effective Design Contract. Approval of a direction does not make an unknown dependency or unreviewed asset production-eligible.

## Completion check

A direction is decision-useful when all of these conditions hold:

- its visual premise is visible in a suitable artifact;
- it is grounded in product intent and references;
- it is tested against relevant content/states/viewports;
- it is specific enough that the owner can accept its trade-offs.

It remains evidence until selected and transferred into approved canon or an effective Design Contract.
